## Five complete coding dimensions

### Oversight

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The draft assigns approval and oversight roles to the Apex Body and court AI Committees, and requires court AI registers, incident databases, and periodic audits. It also provides for review of systems already in use.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> All Court AI Systems and AI Tools shall undergo periodic technical, legal and ethical audits at intervals not exceeding one year

Printed p. 21, Regulation 38(1). [Read the original](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2026/06/2026060342.pdf).

### Evaluation

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: Before approving an AI system, the Appropriate Authority would require a Technical and Ethical Impact Assessment covering matters including purpose, data, bias, error, security, explainability, and redress. The Authority may also direct controlled-environment testing before deployment.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> require the submission of a comprehensive Technical and Ethical Impact Assessment.

Printed p. 20, Regulation 35(1). [Read the original](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2026/06/2026060342.pdf).

### Procurement

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The draft would require prior written approval before private entities provide services connected with court AI systems, with proposals evaluated for technical capability, legal compliance, ethics, security, and financial standing. It also specifies contractual safeguards, audit rights, and reporting obligations.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> No private entity, vendor, or third-party service provider shall undertake, participate in, or provide any service in connection with an AI System deployed in Court processes without the prior written approval

Printed p. 24, Regulation 46(1). [Read the original](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2026/06/2026060342.pdf).

### Redress

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: A party harmed by a prohibited AI use could apply to the court where the system was used, which would hear the matter and pass appropriate orders. The draft preserves other remedies available under law; it does not establish a general grievance route for every AI-related harm.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> Where any harm is caused to any party to a proceeding as a direct or indirect effect of prohibited use of AI under regulation 20

Printed p. 28, Regulation 52(1). [Read the original](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2026/06/2026060342.pdf).

### Accountability and data

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The draft places responsibility for AI-assisted decisions on the responsible officer and treats AI outputs as advisory, subject to verification with stated exceptions. It also requires data-protection safeguards, heightened protection for sensitive judicial data, and recording and reporting of AI incidents.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> Accountability for all decisions made by any officer with the assistance of AI shall rest exclusively upon such officer

Printed p. 8, Regulation 8(1); data provisions at Regulations 10 and 48. [Read the original](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2026/06/2026060342.pdf).
