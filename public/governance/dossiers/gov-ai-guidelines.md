## Five complete coding dimensions

### Oversight

Textual provision: The Committee recommends establishing an AI Governance Group (AIGG), supported by a Technology & Policy Expert Committee, to coordinate cross-government policy. Sectoral agencies and regulators are described as leading monitoring and enforcement in their domains; these are recommendations and role descriptions, not evidence that the proposed AIGG has been established.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> The Committee recommends the creation of an AI Governance Group (AIGG) to develop and oversee India’s position and strategy on AI governance.

Printed p. 34, §2.6, A. AI Governance Group. [Read the original](https://www.psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/publication/India%20Al%20Governance%20Guidelines%205%C2%A0Nov%C2%A02025.pdf).

### Evaluation

Textual provision: The Guidelines recommend an India-specific risk assessment and classification framework and propose that the AI Safety Institute research risks and test and evaluate AI systems. They also recommend developing an AI incident database; the text does not establish that these proposed mechanisms are operational.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> The AISI should be involved in research, risk assessment, and capacity-building. It should test and evaluate AI systems for risks

Printed p. 36, §2.6, B. AI Safety Institute. [Read the original](https://www.psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/publication/India%20Al%20Governance%20Guidelines%205%C2%A0Nov%C2%A02025.pdf).

### Procurement

Context / topic, not a concrete control: The text recommends training public officials to manage public procurements effectively. It does not set out AI-specific procurement standards or mandatory vendor-selection requirements.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> to manage public procurements effectively

Printed p. 17, §2.2, Capacity Building, Recommendations. [Read the original](https://www.psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/publication/India%20Al%20Governance%20Guidelines%205%C2%A0Nov%C2%A02025.pdf).

### Redress

Textual provision: The practical guidance recommends that organisations create grievance mechanisms for AI-related harms, with resolution within a reasonable timeframe. Elsewhere, it recommends accessible channels in multiple languages and formats; these are recommendations, not evidence of an enacted general redress system.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> Create a grievance redressal mechanism to enable reporting of AI-related harms and ensure resolution of such issues within a reasonable timeframe.

Printed p. 42, Part 4, Guidelines for industry. [Read the original](https://www.psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/publication/India%20Al%20Governance%20Guidelines%205%C2%A0Nov%C2%A02025.pdf).

### Accountability and data

Textual provision: The Committee recommends graded liability proportionate to function, anticipated risk, and due diligence, alongside transparency and accountability measures. The text also describes consent, purpose limitation, and data minimisation under the DPDP Act as relevant to AI training and deployment; this records the text’s account of existing law, not a new requirement created by these Guidelines.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> The DPDP Act introduces obligations of consent, purpose limitation, and data minimisation that have direct bearing on AI model training and deployment.

Printed p. 51, Annexure 3, Overview of current laws in India relevant to AI systems. [Read the original](https://www.psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/publication/India%20Al%20Governance%20Guidelines%205%C2%A0Nov%C2%A02025.pdf).
