## Five complete coding dimensions

### Oversight

Textual provision: The Committee recommends that regulated entities adopt a board-approved AI policy, classify AI use cases by risk, and assign governance and risk oversight responsibilities. These are recommendations in the report, not established binding requirements.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> REs should establish a board-approved AI policy

p. 55, § 4.4.46, Recommendation 14. [Read the original](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF).

### Evaluation

Textual provision: The Committee recommends AI-specific evaluations within product approval processes and risk-based audits across data inputs, models, and outputs. It also recommends lifecycle red teaming and ongoing monitoring; the report does not establish that these practices have been implemented.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> REs should implement a comprehensive, risk-based, calibrated AI audit framework

p. 65, § 4.4.75, Recommendation 24. [Read the original](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF).

### Procurement

Textual provision: The report discusses third-party AI dependencies and recommends addressing AI-specific risks and disclosure of vendor or subcontractor AI use in relevant outsourcing agreements. It does not set out a vendor-selection or procurement mandate; the suggested contract changes in Annexure IV are proposals.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> Specific clauses setting out an obligation to disclose the use of AI by third-party vendors and their subcontractors may be incorporated, as applicable, into the Outsourcing Agreement.

p. 80, Annexure IV, item 1. [Read the original](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF).

### Redress

Textual provision: The Committee recommends informing customers when they interact with AI, offering access to human representatives, and providing accessible grievance and recourse mechanisms for AI interactions or decisions. These are report recommendations, not evidence of an enacted redress rule.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> Consumers must be explicitly informed whenever they are interacting with AI systems and should always have the option to switch to human representatives when they want.

p. 58, § 4.4.54. [Read the original](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF).

### Accountability and data

Textual provision: The report says deploying entities should remain accountable for AI outcomes and recommends data-lifecycle governance covering collection, access, use, retention, and deletion. These statements are part of the Committee's principles and recommendations, not a verified adopted obligation.

Force: Recommendation or guidance; not a new enacted obligation. Implementation independently verified: no.

> Entities that deploy AI should be responsible and remain fully accountable for the decisions and outcomes that arise from the use of these systems

p. 37, § 4.3, Sutra 5; data governance recommendation at p. 56, § 4.4.47. [Read the original](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF).
