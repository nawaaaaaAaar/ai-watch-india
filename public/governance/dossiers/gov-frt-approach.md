## Five complete coding dimensions

### Oversight

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The paper recommends that organisations deploying FRT establish an expert ethics committee with autonomy to set guidance, oversee compliance, and address standards and accountability. This is a recommendation in a draft discussion paper, not an established oversight requirement.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> such committees are constituted and given adequate autonomy to prescribe guidelines and codes of practice to ensure compliance with RAI principles.

Printed p. 42, Part II, E.1.B(b), Policy reform. [Read the original](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-11/Ai_for_All_2022_02112022.pdf).

### Evaluation

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The paper recommends independent, regular monitoring of FRT performance and use, including assessment against defined criteria; it also recommends ongoing evaluation and disclosure of overall and demographic error rates. These are proposed safeguards, not evidence that such evaluation is in operation.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> The performance and use of the facial recognition system must be monitored by governmental and non-governmental independent agencies regularly against a set of defined criteria

Printed p. 47, Part II, E.3(c)(iii), Recommendations for procurement. [Read the original](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-11/Ai_for_All_2022_02112022.pdf).

### Procurement

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The paper recommends transparent procurement, public disclosure of criteria and processes, detailed problem-focused RFPs, prior risk and impact assessment, and disclosure of error rates. These are recommendations for future procurement, not adopted procurement rules or mandatory vendor-selection criteria.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> The procurement of the facial recognition technology must be carried out in a transparent manner with periodic public disclosures of the criteria and processes followed.

Printed p. 46, Part II, E.3(a)(i), Recommendations for procurement. [Read the original](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-11/Ai_for_All_2022_02112022.pdf).

### Redress

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The paper recommends an accessible grievance-redress system for FRT, including adjudication of problems such as inaccurate outcomes that deny access. It suggests an ombudsman as a possible accessible first point, with escalation depending on the complaint; it does not establish a functioning or binding redress mechanism.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> it is crucial to establish an easy-to-use and accessible grievance redressal system.

Printed p. 48, Part II, E.4(a)(i), Recommendations for Impacted consumers. [Read the original](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-11/Ai_for_All_2022_02112022.pdf).

### Accountability and data

Textual provision: Draft / consultation proposal: The paper recommends a data-protection regime that covers state agencies and biometric data, alongside privacy by design, public-facing documentation of privacy practices, audit trails, and clear accountability arrangements. These are recommendations; the paper does not itself enact data-protection duties or establish liability rules.

Force: Draft proposal; no obligation inferred. Implementation independently verified: no.

> a document explaining the PBD policy and other privacy, and data protection principles used by the developers in developing the AI system must be made publicly accessible.

Printed p. 45, Part II, E.2(d)(i), Recommendations for developers and vendors. [Read the original](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-11/Ai_for_All_2022_02112022.pdf).
