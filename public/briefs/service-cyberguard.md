## I4C CyberGuard: I4C CyberGuard: decision-accountability case

Collection: service. Comparison: System claim to accountability evidence. Type: Public-service case. Actor/class: Cybercrime administration. Earlier context: Hackathon explanation.

Status: Official integration reported.

### Textual observation

Government reporting says the shortlisted classifier was integrated with I4C systems; the hackathon results identify teams but do not identify an executed production contract. [Hackathon explanation](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817&reg=3&lang=1) [29 July release, CyberGuard paragraph](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2291062&reg=48&lang=1)

Earlier excerpt (SYSTEM RECORD, as extracted):

> The Hackathon resulted in the development of AI-based solutions to enhance the classification of cybercrime complaints and support the identification of emerging crime patterns, trends, and modus operandi on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP).

Later excerpt (SUPPORTING RECORD, as printed, as extracted):

> The shortlisted solution has been integrated with I4C systems.

### Analyst interpretation

Complaint classification may influence routing and prioritisation, but the records do not establish that a model decides FIR registration or rejects a complaint. Research metrics from a different hackathon entry cannot be transferred to the production system.

### Limits and follow-up

Winner selection is not proof of the deployed supplier or a service contract. Integration does not establish measured operational accuracy, complaint outcomes, or automatic adverse decisions.

Reporting question: Which decision can this system influence, what evidence supports its use, and how can a person obtain human review?
