# AI Watch India: Public-Service Decision Accountability

Six bounded investigations, all twelve accountability dimensions per case. Checked 5 October 2026, India date. The contribution is a traceable route from deployment claims to the records needed for reporting and decisions, not an AI adoption directory or a claim to have audited every Indian system.

## What this edition contributes

This edition extends the policy-change and implementation desk to systems that can affect access to examinations, handling of complaints, access to court judgments, judicial research, clinical consultations and policing. It includes six substantive case briefs, not 72 inflated “contributions”: the 72 rows are a completeness register for the six investigations.

The collection joins 56 policy comparisons and ten institutional implementation checkpoints. After the edition 05 completeness follow-up, the complete desk contains five collections, 72 evidence units, 46 research briefs, 46 source records and ten directed English corrections. These are repository inventory counts, not counts of operational systems or independently validated successes.

The public-service corpus contains twenty selected source records and reuses one earlier government record. A separate extracted DPDP parent Act is included as statutory context, not another policy comparison. Government self-reporting, tenders, research abstracts, developer accounts, historical guidance, privacy terms, vendor accounts and news reporting are distinguished. A source is evidence of what it states; it is not automatically proof of the stated outcome.

## Consequential findings

### Examination access: a tender and a deployed app are not the same thing

UPSC describes an in-house mobile application developed with NeGD support, deployed across its May 2026 Civil Services preliminary examination venues. [The 4 June implementation release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2268879&reg=3&lang=1) documents the workflow, including invigilators' Android phones.

The later record reports a September 2025 pilot and scaling on 24 May 2026 for about 5.50 lakh candidates. [The 4 October release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseDetail.aspx?PRID=2317336&reg=48&lang=1) supports that reported sequence, not an independent accuracy audit.

A 2024 procurement instrument covers a larger biometric, QR-code and AI-CCTV package; it specifies physical photo checking and cloud-data retention terms. [The UPSC tender and corrigendum](https://upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/Tender-AadhaarQRFaceRecgAI-Corrgn_Eng-180724.pdf) do not themselves establish the contract or retention policy of the later in-house mobile application.

The reporting contribution is this lineage boundary. A journalist can ask for the current mismatch/offline SOP, candidate review route, device lifecycle, executed award lineage and false-rejection/acceptance results, rather than report authentication speed as proof that exclusion errors are solved. These records remain unverified in this corpus; the finding is not that UPSC has no fallback.

### Cybercrime administration: integration is not the production contract

The government records a CyberGuard challenge focused on complaint classification and crime-pattern identification, with a winner table and multiple input formats. [The hackathon results](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817&reg=3&lang=1) establish selection information, not an executed production supply contract.

A later government statement says the shortlisted solution was integrated with I4C systems. [The 29 July government record](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2291062&reg=48&lang=1) supports reported integration, not the identity of every production component or its measured error profile.

The useful next question is how a classification affects routing, prioritisation or investigation, and how an officer or complainant can correct it. The records reviewed do not establish that the model decides FIR registration or rejects complaints. No accuracy result from a different hackathon entry is transferred to the integrated system.

### Judicial translation: a concrete local review process is not a universal safeguard

The official judicial backgrounder describes SUVAS translation, e-SCR hosting and translation committee oversight. [The judicial AI explanation](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226283&reg=48&lang=2) supports assistive translation use, not an automated decision on a case's merits.

An Allahabad notice documents software-translation vetting, correction, certification and a disclaimer requirement, with remuneration tied to English-source words. [The September 2023 notice](https://www.allahabadhighcourt.in/event/event_16290_18-09-2023.pdf) establishes a historical local process, not a current national software budget or uniform correction procedure.

The contribution is a verifiable human-review lead with its jurisdiction and date intact. It does not assume every court uses the same process, or supply disclaimer wording that the reviewed notice does not contain.

### Judicial research: experimental status must survive the headline

The precise SUPACE section of the official explanation calls the tool experimental and not regularly used in judicial work. [The February judicial AI record](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226283&reg=48&lang=2) is more specific than a general headline about AI use.

The June instrument retained here is a consultation draft proposing an assistive, human-primacy approach. [The Supreme Court consultation document](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2026/06/2026060342.pdf) is not treated as enacted regulation or an implemented SUPACE SOP.

SUPACE is deliberately included as an experimental counterexample. The contribution prevents designed capability from being presented as routine deployed judicial decision-making; later routine use and a later final instrument were not verified, which is not proof neither exists.

### Clinical support: a national study abstract is useful, but is not a complete validation dossier

The parliamentary response describes symptom capture, specialist referral through a rule engine and differential diagnosis supplied to the hub doctor, with integration in April 2023 and reported usage through July 2026. [The 7 August parliamentary answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS284_ZZWF4R.pdf?source=lsapps) supports clinician-assistance and usage claims, not autonomous diagnosis or national diagnostic accuracy.

A local ICMR research brief cites 90% validation using 4,401 local records and recommends integration into eSanjeevani. [The local study brief](https://nihrjodhpur.icmr.org.in/uploads/policybriefs/1782386587_1.PolicyBrief_Telemedicine.pdf) therefore cannot supply the existing national deployed CDSS's accuracy score.

The completeness follow-up located a national implementation-study abstract describing a knowledge-based framework, expert-clinician validation and implementation in eSanjeevani 2.0. The retrieved [DOI abstract and declarations](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.22.25340800) are not the full manuscript, which could not be retrieved; they do not establish independent numeric error rates, subgroup performance or later operational safety.

The same study declares that the study received no funding. That statement concerns the study, not the cost of development, hosting or national deployment. [Study funding declaration](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.22.25340800)

The developer describes integration since March 2023, whereas the parliamentary response says April 2023. The ledger retains the difference without assuming the dates identify the same component or milestone. [Developer healthcare account](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/impact/healthcare/) and [parliamentary answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS284_ZZWF4R.pdf?source=lsapps)

Historical telemedicine guidelines prohibit AI/ML platforms from independently counselling or prescribing and require a platform grievance mechanism. These provisions establish historical guidance, not verification of today's CDSS override implementation or a working patient complaint form. [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, sections 5.4 and 5.6](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)

The published privacy terms discuss C-DAC-managed storage and retention tied to account existence and interventions, while excluding treatment-generated medical reports/diagnoses from that clause. [The eSanjeevani privacy policy](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/esanjeevani2.0_Private_Policy.pdf) does not justify one universal retention period.

The same policy provides a privacy-concern contact, not a verified clinical-error appeal mechanism. [The privacy policy's contact provision](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/esanjeevani2.0_Private_Policy.pdf) must not be expanded into a medical-remedy promise.

The broader telemedicine utilisation study raises privacy, access and service concerns. [The NHSRC programme study](https://www.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/Telemedicine%20Final%20Report%202025.pdf) supplies programme context, not a deployed-CDSS clinical safety evaluation.

The contribution is a precise validation boundary and a patient-facing governance question: which deployed component/version was evaluated, who can override it, and where can a patient obtain clinical review? This is research, not clinical advice.

### Policing: retain disputed pilot counts and announced scale

Microsoft's feature describes a Nagpur pilot and announcement of expansion from 23 to 1,100 police stations. [The vendor feature](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/features/a-race-against-time-maharashtra-police-get-an-ai-copilot-to-fight-cybercrime/) establishes the account and announcement, not a government-certified completed rollout.

Another Microsoft page describes 25 pilot stations, CyberEye's product, MARVEL adaptation, Marathi configuration and multimodal complaint inputs. [The customer story](https://www.microsoft.com/en-in/aifirstmovers/FY26CrimeOS) remains vendor material, not an executed procurement record.

The Indian Express explanation contains both 23- and 25-station descriptions. [The news report](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/mahacrimeos-ai-maharashtra-10418600/) does not resolve the count conflict or independently audit performance.

The contribution is to retain conflicting denominators, distinguish expansion intention from completed active use, and expose the missing government authorisation, contract, verification/override, data and redress records. The material reviewed does not prove statewide non-use, unlawful procurement or ineffective performance.

## Method and evidence standard

Each case has the same twelve fields. “Documented” means the reviewed source directly supports the stated point; it does not upgrade self-reporting to independently verified operation. “Partial” means only part of the dimension is supported. “Not verified” means the relevant answer or a critical current-status point was not established in this bounded search. “Conflicting sources” retains materially different source descriptions without choosing a convenient answer.

Quotation membership is checked against normalized extracted source text. Locators are descriptive and jurisdiction/date aware; page numbers are not invented when unavailable. New snapshots contain selected excerpts, are labelled as such, and carry SHA-256 hashes of those UTF-8 files, not original PDF bytes. Full records remain at their original URLs.

Searches cover deployment, procurement, funding, data, evaluation, override, privacy and redress for every case. The accompanying search log records exact query scope and retrieval problems. No requests, interviews, complaints, source corrections or registry confirmations were undertaken.

All six cases are present in the matrix even when a field is unverified. Missing information is neither silently dropped nor converted to a risk score. The included evidence supports reporting questions; it does not establish individualized rights, legal compliance, clinical safety or causal harm.

## How a journalist or decision-maker can use this

- **Report preparation:** select a case and export the full matrix, quotes, source types, caveats and record-request checklist. Verify the full source and seek an institution's response before publishing allegations.
- **Procurement review:** ask for the executed award, current version, intended decision role, acceptance test, override procedure and data-processing terms. Do not substitute an old tender for the current implementation.
- **Policy scrutiny:** compare announced institutions and recommended safeguards with system-specific operational records. General AI guidelines are not evidence that each deployment implements their recommendations.
- **Personal decision support:** use documented contacts and procedures only within their stated scope. An unverified AI-specific route is not a finding that ordinary administrative, court, health or policing remedies are unavailable.
- **Further research:** resolve specific missing records, then update source dates and claims rather than adding news cards without evidence.

## Limits and completion boundary

This edition completes a curated six-case research-and-website expansion, with all 72 dimensions retained and usable exports. It is not exhaustive coverage of every Indian public-service AI deployment, a representative prevalence estimate, independent legal/clinical review, a live monitor or a guarantee that records have not changed.

There is no public website launch or paid service provisioning. The website has no accounts, analytics, model calls or remote notebook uploads; notes remain session-local unless exported by the user. Original policy collections and their limitations remain intact.

The following register supplies every case field, its evidence and unresolved record queue. It is the inspectable basis for the six case briefs, not a substitute for full source documents.
